\section{Reference model and score}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Reproduce the benchmark pipeline (Appendix~\ref{app:benchmark}).
    \item Out-of-fold score: 5 stratified folds, $R$ seeds, whole pipeline refitted in each fold. (we use out of fold pz as a sort of pre validation set per model extension)
    \item Why out-of-fold: in-sample scores are optimistic and would bias the test against $\mathbf{Hd}$. So, we train and test the benchmark and obtain an OOF score per patient which summarizes the prediction of the model on unseen patient (average of the p assigned to each patient in each repetition): 
\end{itemize}
\begin{equation}
    \bar{p}_i = \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R} \hat{p}_i^{(r)},
    \qquad
    S_{B,i} = \operatorname{logit}(\bar{p}_i)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Models}
\begin{align}
    M_0:\;& \operatorname{logit} P(D_i = 1) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 S_{B,i} \\
    M_1:\;& \operatorname{logit} P(D_i = 1) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 S_{B,i} + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\top}\mathbf{Hd}_i

$M_0$ is the recalibrated benchmark (recalibration is required for comparing two models discrimination, otherwise we mix the effects) \citeneeded{Cox 1958}; $M_1$ extends it with $\mathbf{Hd}$ \cite{janssen_updating_2008}.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Calibration} of both models: intercept and slope \cite{alba_discrimination_2017}. 
    \item \textbf{Likelihood ratio test} on the $\mathbf{Hd}$ block \cite{pepe_testing_2013}:
    \begin{equation}
        H_0:\; \boldsymbol{\gamma} = \mathbf{0},
        \qquad
        \Lambda = 2\left(\ell_1 - \ell_0\right) \;\overset{H_0}{\sim}\; \chi^2_{p}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Performance gain} with bootstrap CI: $\Delta$AUC, $\Delta$Brier, $\Delta$net benefit. Pepe 2013 proved that if we reject H0 we confirm also a statistically significant increase in $\Delta$AUC, but we still need to QUANTIFY the improvement. this is what performance gain is for.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Secondary analysis (Q2)}

\paragraph{Model structure.}
Regional features average the indices over each region, so they lose where on the wall the values are.
Q2 keeps this information by describing each patient as a set of $N$ wall points, each with its coordinates and its indices,
$\mathcal{P}_i = \{(\mathbf{x}_{ij}, \mathbf{q}_{ij})\}_{j=1}^{N}$.
A small PointNet++ encoder $E_\phi$ \citeneeded{Qi et al.\ 2017, PointNet++}, the same family of networks used by Peng et al.\ \citeneeded{Peng et al.\ 2026},
maps the whole set to one vector $\mathbf{z}_i = E_\phi(\mathcal{P}_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$:
it applies the same layers to local neighbourhoods of points and then pools them, so the result does not depend on the order of the points but does depend on how values are arranged in space.
The encoder is trained as an autoencoder, without the outcome: a decoder $D_\psi$ receives $\mathbf{z}_i$ and a wall coordinate $\mathbf{x}_{ij}$ and must reconstruct the indices at that point,
$\hat{\mathbf{q}}_{ij} = D_\psi(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{x}_{ij})$, minimising
$\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{nN}\sum_{i,j} \lVert \hat{\mathbf{q}}_{ij} - \mathbf{q}_{ij} \rVert^2_{\mathbf{W}}$.
Because the decoder already gets the geometry, $\mathbf{z}_i$ must encode how the hemodynamic field is distributed on the wall, not the shape itself.
With few events, 32 dimensions are too many for a logistic model: $\mathbf{z}_i$ is averaged over point resamplings and reduced with PCA to $k = 1$--$3$ components,
$\mathbf{Z}_i = \mathbf{V}_k^{\top}(\bar{\mathbf{z}}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu})$.
$\mathbf{Z}_i$ then enters the same logistic framework as Q1, as one more block of predictors:
\begin{equation}
    M_3:\; \operatorname{logit} P(D_i = 1) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 S_{B,i} + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\top}\mathbf{Hd}_i + \boldsymbol{\delta}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_i
\end{equation}
In this way the spatial information is learned without labels, while the predictive step stays a small logistic model, directly comparable with $M_1$.
Architecture and training details are in Appendix~\ref{app:encoder}.

\paragraph{Hypotheses and tests.}
The main test is $M_3$ against $M_1$, i.e.\ whether $\mathbf{Z}$ adds information beyond $S_B$ and $\mathbf{Hd}$:
\begin{equation}
    H_0^{(2)}:\; \boldsymbol{\delta} = \mathbf{0},
    \qquad
    \Lambda_2 = 2\left(\ell_3 - \ell_1\right) \;\overset{H_0}{\sim}\; \chi^2_{k}
\end{equation}
As a secondary test, $M_2$ ($S_B + \mathbf{Z}$) is compared with $M_0$.
The order is the same as in the primary analysis: calibration, likelihood ratio test, performance gain with bootstrap CI.
Encoder and PCA are fitted once on the full cohort for the test, and refitted inside each fold for the performance estimates.
Q1 and Q2 are corrected together with Holm--Bonferroni.
Before testing, the encoder must pass two checks: reconstruction $R^2$ on unseen patients (otherwise Q2 stops),
and a linear probe $\mathbf{Hd}_i \approx \mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_i + \mathbf{b}$ showing that $\mathbf{z}$ contains at least the information in $\mathbf{Hd}$.
Two controls separate hemodynamics from shape and from artefacts: $\mathbf{Z}_{\text{shape}}$, the same encoder trained on geometric descriptors only, and $\mathbf{Z}_{\text{perm}}$, with fields assigned to another patient's geometry (expected no gain).
Each component is decoded on a reference geometry, $\hat{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{x}) = D_\psi(\boldsymbol{\mu} + a\,\sigma_c\,\mathbf{v}_c, \mathbf{x})$ with $a \in \{-2, 0, 2\}$, to show which spatial pattern it encodes.
If $M_3$ improves on $M_1$ and $\mathbf{Z}$ beats $\mathbf{Z}_{\text{shape}}$, the spatial distribution of hemodynamics adds information; if not, regional means are enough, or the gain comes from shape.
